Once there are more pages than a sidebar can hold, search is how the site feels. A bad list makes a careful library look like a junk drawer. I treat it as a surface, not a box I bolt on after the pages exist.

Frontend Atlas and this journal stay mostly static. The index is its own payload: stable id, title, summary, tags, search terms, route. I don't want every article body downloaded so someone can type three letters. Ranking only sees the index.

Search terms exist because I name a thing one way and a reader types another. Extra recall, not a hidden keyword dump. Title and summary are what a person actually reads in the row. An exact title should beat an accidental mention. A tag hit can beat a common word buried in a long summary. Same query, same order, every visit. If the order wobbles I can't tell whether a tweak worked.

The scorer stays boring. A few field weights. I look at queries that should hit and ones that shouldn't, and I move a weight when a real mismatch shows up. More constants isn't a better engine while the corpus still fits in one JSON file.

Keyboard is the product, not a nicety. Open, type, move, activate, leave. No pointer. Focus doesn't jump when the list shrinks. Empty query, no matches, and "index still loading" are three different states. A blank panel is me failing to say which one.

Reduced motion stays reduced. On a small screen the list scrolls inside the surface, and the surface has an exit. A modal you can't dismiss is slower than just scrolling the index.

What I'd actually measure: a handful of real queries, whether the top hit is the one I meant, whether keyboard alone finishes it, index bytes, time to first results. This corpus does not need a search cluster. If it grows a lot, then a precomputed index or a server. Not before the payload says so.

The ranking weights are intentionally uneven: exact title tokens score `32`, aliases `25`, tags `21`, summary `10`, and body mentions `2`. A full-title phrase match adds another `55` (or `100` for an exact title). So a query that names an article should beat an incidental body mention; the implementation, not a search service, makes that decision.
