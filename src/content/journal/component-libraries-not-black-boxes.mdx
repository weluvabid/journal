A dialog is not a box with an X. A combobox is not an input taped to a list. If I can't explain the keyboard contract, I can't compose the thing, and I can't debug it when focus vanishes.

That's the standard for Iris. I build on shadcn-svelte and I keep their primitives. I'm not rewriting focus traps to feel like I own the stack. I am also not shipping a button whose behaviour I can't describe.

What belongs to the primitive: focus trap, dismissal, listbox keyboard map, the roles. What belongs to the app: the title, the items, what they mean. Tokens and spacing can move. They don't get to delete the focus ring or the accessible name while they're at it.

When I'm unsure, I don't wire the component into a full page and hope. One file. Render it. Tab. Escape. Look at the DOM. Ask one question: can the parent control it, does focus return, does the name survive composition, does theming eat the outline? A tiny proof answers that. A pretty gallery hides it.

Rewriting every primitive throws away years of edge cases. Adopting a library you can't inspect creates behaviour nobody on the project owns. The middle is: keep the hard interaction in the primitive, own composition and product styling, and have a checklist if you ever do replace it.

A component is reusable when the API says what it is responsible for, the accessibility behaviour is known, the deps are deliberate, and the failure is testable. "Looks fine in the screenshot" is not that.
